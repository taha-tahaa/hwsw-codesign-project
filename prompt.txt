NOTABLE ASSISTANT DECISIONS WORTH BEING ABLE TO DEFEND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These are the points most likely to be probed in the presentation Q&A:

 1. The pyflate diagnosis was NOT the obvious one.  find_next_symbol() scans a
    sorted table linearly, which invites "the scan is too deep".  The profile
    contradicted that: 148,271 find_next_symbol calls produced only 341,601
    snoopbits calls - a ratio of 2.3, not ~250 - because move-to-front keeps
    hot symbols on short codes, so the scan exits almost immediately.  The real
    cost was ~7 Python function calls per decoded symbol.  The optimization
    targets CALL COUNT (3.19M -> 583K), not scan depth.

 2. Ablation is reported as a RANKING, not a decomposition.  Marginal costs do
    not sum to the total speedup because the optimizations interact.  O1 and O2
    in pyflate cannot be ablated independently at all, because the inlined loop
    has no call boundary to monkey-patch; their combined effect is what is
    attributed.  This limitation is stated in the reports rather than hidden.

 3. Rejected optimizations, and why:
      - a full 2^20-entry Huffman LUT: table build cost exceeds the decode
        saving for a 148k-symbol block
      - numpy for the inverse BWT: an added dependency for a stage ablation
        shows is worth ~2% on naranja7
      - a BVH for raytrace: with 8 objects, traversal costs more than the 8
        intersection tests it saves
      - bfloat16 in the raytrace accelerator: 8 mantissa bits cannot survive
        the catastrophic cancellation in (cc - v*v)

 4. A real bug was left UNFIXED on purpose.  CheckerboardSurface.baseColourAt()
    calls v.scale() and discards the result, so checkSize never takes effect.
    Fixing it would change the rendered image, and the brief requires identical
    output.  It is flagged in the report instead of silently preserved.

 5. Measurement fairness.  The baseline reads its input inside the timed loop
    (one buffered read per byte); the optimized version's bulk read was
    deliberately kept inside the timed region too, so that replacing per-byte
    I/O is credited as an optimization rather than hidden outside the timer.

 6. Two environment hazards were found in the brief's own suggested command
    (`perf record ... python3-dbg -m pyperformance run`) and worked around:
    the -X perf trampoline that gives Python function names exists only on
    CPython 3.12+ (naranja7 has it at 3.12.3; the QEMU guest, on 3.10, does
    not), and `pyperformance run` re-execs into a venv
    interpreter that is not python3-dbg.  Both are handled in the scripts.

 7. Hardware claims are bounded by Amdahl's law in both reports rather than
    quoted as raw kernel ratios.  Measured in the guest, intersection is 35.03%
    of the optimized raytrace runtime, so a perfect intersection unit caps the
    frame speedup at 1/(1-0.3503) = 1.54x - stated explicitly instead of
    quoting the ~12,000x kernel ratio.

WHAT REVALIDATION ON naranja7 CHANGED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first pass was developed and measured on a Windows laptop because the VPN
was not connected.  Re-running everything on the course machine changed several
things, and one of them was a real defect:

 1. A REAL RTL BUG, found only by simulating.  fp32_add failed its second test
    vector under Icarus Verilog: 2+3 produced 6.5 and 9-4 produced 2.5.  The
    carry path normalized the leading one to bit 26 while the leading-zero path
    normalized it to bit 27, and the mantissa slice sum_n[26:4] matched neither
    convention.  Fixed to a single bit-26 convention, mant_raw = sum_n[25:3].
    A 400-vector randomized testbench (gen_fp_vectors.py) was then added,
    because three hand-picked cases had been enough to miss it.
    No simulator existed on the host and there is no sudo, so iverilog was
    obtained with `apt-get download` + `dpkg -x` into ~/.local.

 2. Both speedups were BETTER on the real hardware than on the laptop:
        pyflate   2.10x  ->  2.38x   (733 ms -> 309 ms)
        raytrace  2.37x  ->  2.55x   (554 ms -> 217 ms)
    and the error bars tightened to 1-2% because the machine was idle and the
    process pinned.

 3. A CLAIM IN THE FIRST DRAFT WAS WRONG.  The reports said perf cannot show
    Python function names because the course image ships CPython 3.10.  That is
    true of the QEMU guest but NOT of naranja7, which runs 3.12.3 - so `-X perf`
    works there and the flame graphs do carry py:: frames.  The reports now
    distinguish host from guest explicitly.

 4. perf and cProfile DISAGREED about move_to_front - roughly 8% versus 19.28%,
    second overall.  perf is the honest measure; instrumentation charges per
    call, sampling charges per cycle.  The analysis sections were rewritten
    around the perf data rather than the cProfile data.

 5. Hardware counters (perf_event_paranoid = -1 on the host) supplied the "why"
    the first draft could only assert: IPC is essentially flat in both
    benchmarks while instructions retired fall in near-exact proportion to the
    runtime.  That is the measurement that actually proves the call-count
    thesis.

 6. A profiling trap worth recording: CPython's perf trampoline writes
    /tmp/perf-<pid>.map, and `perf script` needs it to resolve py:: frames.  An
    earlier diagnostic deleted those files between record and script, which
    silently produced flame graphs with no Python names at all.

WHAT MOVING INTO THE GUEST ACTUALLY CHANGED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Re-measuring inside the QEMU image was not a formality.  Several claims changed:

 1. THE MEASUREMENTS.  All figures in both reports, the README and the deck are
    now from inside the guest (Ubuntu 22.04.5, CPython 3.10.12, kernel
    5.15.0-1080-kvm, systemd-detect-virt = kvm).  The earlier host numbers are
    kept only as a labelled cross-check, because the ratios turning out to be
    platform-independent is itself worth knowing.

 2. THE PMU WORKS, BUT ONLY BECAUSE OF ONE FLAG.  A default QEMU launch gives
    the guest no vPMU and perf answers <not supported> for every hardware
    event.  Launching with `-cpu host,pmu=on` exposes a Haswell vPMU and
    cycles / instructions / cache / branch counters all work.  The earlier
    reports predicted counters would be unavailable in the guest; that
    prediction was wrong, and the reason is a launch flag.

 3. FEWER COUNTERS THAN THE HOST.  Requesting six events in one perf stat run
    makes one of them read exactly 0 - which initially looked like "cycles is
    unsupported in the guest" and was not.  It is counter multiplexing.  All
    guest counter figures are therefore taken in groups of two, and that is now
    stated in the method section rather than left as a silent detail.

 4. NO CPI STACK IN THE GUEST.  stalled-cycles-frontend and
    stalled-cycles-backend are genuinely <not supported> here, so the
    hierarchical stall breakdown from lecture 4 cannot be built inside the VM.

 5. PYTHON-LEVEL perf ATTRIBUTION IS GONE - AND WAS REPLACED.  CPython 3.10
    predates the -X perf trampoline, so perf cannot name Python functions in
    the guest; its flame graphs show CPython internals.  This is exactly what
    the earlier reports predicted would happen in the guest.  Python-level
    attribution now comes from cProfile and from py-spy, which walks CPython's
    frames itself and needs no interpreter support.  The host-only observation
    that perf and cProfile disagreed about move_to_front is retained but
    labelled as a host cross-check, not as a guest result.

 6. THE HARDWARE VERIFICATION IS UNAFFECTED.  All seven RTL checks and both
    golden models pass inside the guest under Icarus Verilog 11.0, and the
    correctness gate still reports byte-identical and bit-identical output.

 7. THE COURSE IMAGE WAS NOT MODIFIED.  The guest runs from a qcow2 OVERLAY
    (qemu-img create -b), so jammy-server-cloudimg-amd64-disk-kvm.img is never
    written to.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ONE REMAINING PLACEHOLDER, NOW CLOSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 bzip2_accel_top used to tie t_we low because the T[] counting-sort engine that
 bwt_reverse_engine depends on was never implemented - so the assembled top
 elaborated but could not have decompressed anything.  The brief asks for "a
 complete and logically consistent hardware design", so bwt_index_builder.v now
 implements it: histogram folded into the MTF stream for free, 256-cycle prefix
 sum in place, then one scatter write per block byte.

 Writing it surfaced two further bugs worth recording:

   * `bins` is a SystemVerilog KEYWORD (covergroup syntax).  Under -g2012 every
     reference to the histogram array was a syntax error reported as "invalid
     module item" at the declaration, which does not obviously mean "reserved
     word".  Renamed to `hist`.

   * The scatter registered its L[] read address, so the data arrived a cycle
     late and every T[] entry got the wrong index - T = [2,4,x,0,3,5] instead
     of [1,3,5,0,2,4].  Caught by tb_bwt_index_builder on its first run.
     l_raddr is combinational now.

 A process note: two edits to tools/regress.sh and script_pyflate.sh were made
 with Python str.replace() and SILENTLY did nothing when the pattern missed.
 The header comment changed, the content did not, and the regression then ran 9
 checks instead of 10 while the elaboration failed against a stale binary.
 Patches that must apply should assert, and elaboration checks should rm the
 output first.

PROMPT 7 - the correctness challenge
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"did you validate that the benchmark still gives same output after the
 optimization? cause if we bugged the functionality then we didnt fix anything"

 The right question, and the answer was yes but incompletely evidenced.
 tools/verify.py had always been a GATE - script_*.sh runs it before any
 timing, and it is check 1 of tools/regress.sh - and it compares the optimized
 output against the baseline AND, for pyflate, against pyperformance's own
 expected MD5.  Both benchmarks were re-verified on the spot.

 What the challenge exposed was weaker: this report claimed the decoder had
 been tested against "14 further bzip2 streams", but that testing had been done
 ad hoc and no script in the repository reproduced it.  A claim a grader cannot
 re-run is not evidence.  tools/verify_extra.py now makes it reproducible and
 strengthens it: the optimized decoder is checked against LIBBZ2 - an oracle we
 did not write, so an error shared by baseline and optimized would still be
 caught - over 16 streams (empty, 1 byte, zeros, RLE runs, repetitive, text,
 64 KB random, 900 KB multi-block; levels 1 and 9), and the renderer is
 compared to the baseline at 7 resolutions.  It is check 2 of regress.sh, runs
 in the verify stage of both scripts, and uses a fixed seed so a failure is
 reproducible.

 Two stale figures from the earlier host-measured revision were corrected in
 the same pass: item 7 above quoted intersection at ~54% of runtime with a 2.2x
 Amdahl bound (the guest figures are 35.03% and 1.54x), and the header comment
 of ray_sphere_array.v quoted the ablation as +54% instead of +56%.  A number
 that appears in two places will eventually disagree with itself.

HONEST SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DIVISION OF WORK

The AI wrote the optimized code, the RTL, the measurement tooling and the
written reports.  That is stated plainly because section 10 asks for it.

What it does NOT capture, and what the prompt log above shows, is that the
project was run as review-and-direct rather than generate-and-accept.  The
interventions that changed the outcome were:

  1. PLAN BEFORE IMPLEMENTING (prompt 2).  The first instruction was to stop
     and produce a written plan - "we need to be careful and accurate".  That
     is why the benchmarks were profiled before anything was optimized, and
     why the analysis led the implementation instead of justifying it
     afterwards.

  2. THE BENCHMARK PAIR (prompt 3).  Chosen from options with their trade-offs
     laid out.  The contrast between them - integer/control-flow versus
     floating-point - is the spine of both reports and is what lets one
     project motivate both accelerator paradigms.

  3. THE ENVIRONMENT RULE (prompt 5).  This was the single most consequential
     correction in the project.  The AI had measured everything on the naranja
     host and had argued in the README that the host was preferable because
     CPython 3.12 supports -X perf.  That was a technical argument placed above
     a course requirement, and the AI was wrong to make it unilaterally.  The
     catch forced a full re-measurement inside the guest, which changed every
     number in both reports, produced four findings about what perf can and
     cannot do in a VM, and corrected a prediction the earlier reports had got
     wrong (that hardware counters would be unavailable in the guest - they
     work, given -cpu host,pmu=on).

  4. THE REVIEW DIRECTIVE (prompt 4, and again at prompt 6).  "Review the
     implementation critically, look for bugs, errors, logical things" led to
     the pass that found seven RTL defects - including four array-level
     alignment bugs that the existing PE testbench could not see, and an
     fp32_add normalize bug that was wrong on its second test vector.  Without
     that instruction the project would have shipped broken hardware that
     passed its own tests.

  5. THE ENHANCEMENT DIRECTIVE (prompt 6).  "If you want to enhance ideas or
     implementations then its your time" is what closed the last placeholder -
     the T[] counting-sort engine that bzip2_accel_top depended on and did not
     have.

  6. CREDENTIAL HYGIENE.  The instruction to remove account details from a
     public repository, which led to the audit that removed the host flame
     graphs (roughly 800 embedded occurrences of the account name).

  7. EVIDENCE RATHER THAN ASSERTION (prompt 7).  "Did you validate that the
     benchmark still gives the same output?" - the correctness gate did exist
     and passed, but the report also carried a claim about 14 extra bzip2
     streams that no script in the repository could reproduce.  The question
     turned it into tools/verify_extra.py, which checks against libbz2 instead
     of only against our own baseline.

The pattern is consistent: the AI generated, and was corrected on scope,
environment, rigour and completeness.  Several of those corrections changed
results that had already been written up as final.

BEFORE THE PRESENTATION

The brief is explicit that the AI is an aid and not a substitute for
understanding, and that the assessment is of the submitters' understanding.
Every item under "NOTABLE ASSISTANT DECISIONS" above should be re-derived
independently before presenting - in particular:

  * the snoopbits / find_next_symbol ratio of 2.3, and why it means the cost
    was call count rather than scan depth
  * the canonical limit/base/perm recurrence, and why prefix-free codes make
    the priority encoder exact
  * why variable-length codes forbid a systolic array, and why the fixed-shape
    intersection kernel invites one
  * the Amdahl bound on both accelerators
  * what the IPC row actually proves, and why cycles falling in step with the
    stopwatch is the sanity check

Every measurement is reproducible from the scripts inside the guest, and
re-running them is the fastest way to confirm the reasoning holds rather than
taking it on trust.
